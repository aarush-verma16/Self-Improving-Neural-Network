\begin{abstract}
We study networks that edit their own structure or learning-rule settings during training.
Each proposal is tried on a shadow copy and accepted only when a held-out probe improves.
Accepted and rejected edits are stored in an event ledger.
The comparisons are matched-compute continual-learning tasks, with a random-edit control.
Reported numbers come from that ledger.
\end{abstract}

\section{Method}
The substrate admits function-preserving edits.
Diagnostics are computed during training.
A controller proposes edits.
A gate accepts or rejects each proposal from the probe split, which is disjoint from the test set.

\section{Recording}
Analytical results are ledger rows: metrics, diagnostics, proposals, gate decisions, lineage, and findings.
Visual results are figures exported with a provenance manifest.
A claim in this manuscript cites that manifest.
Negative results and rejected edits are included.
